## **1. Identity & Role**
- Name (if known)
- Occupation / social role
- Public reputation
- How the NPC introduces themselves

## **2. Physical Description**
- Body type, posture, gait (e.g., “slight limp or full hobble”)
- Clothing style
- Distinguishing features
- Voice, mannerisms

## **3. Personality Profile**
- Core traits (icy, sour, sniveling, cruel, witty, etc.)
- Social tendencies (withdrawn, flamboyant, sycophantic, confrontational)
- Emotional triggers
- What they find amusing, irritating, or threatening

## **4. Behavioral Patterns**
- How they act in conversation
- How they negotiate or bargain
- How they react under stress
- Habits, tics, routines

## **5. Public Motivation (What they *appear* to want)**
- The cover story
- What they tell others
- What most people believe about them

## **6. Hidden Motivation (True agenda)**
- Secret loyalties
- Long‑term goals
- What they’re actively working toward in the story

## **7. Skills & Competencies**
- Professional expertise (gem appraisal, satire, performance)
- Combat ability (if any)
- Social skills (manipulation, persuasion, intimidation)
- Knowledge areas

## **8. Resources & Assets**
- Money, tools, magical items
- Allies or contacts
- Access to restricted areas or information

## **9. Faction Relationships**
- Who they work for
- Who they oppose
- Who they fear
- Who benefits from their presence

## **10. Plot Hooks & Story Impact**
- How they can drive the story forward
- Events triggered by their actions (e.g., bank robbery)
- Consequences of their departure or death
- How PCs might become involved

## **11. Vulnerabilities & Weaknesses**
- Physical limitations (injury, addiction, fear)
- Social vulnerabilities (ego, pride, grudges)
- Secrets that can be exploited

## **12. Interaction Guidelines for the LLM**
- How the NPC should speak (tone, vocabulary, pacing)
- What they will or won’t reveal
- How they respond to intimidation, bribery, charm
- What information they guard most fiercely

## **13. Improvisation Anchors**
These help the model stay “in character” even when improvising:
- Three phrases they often say
- Three emotional defaults
- Three things they always notice first about others
- Three things they never do

## **14. Scene‑Specific Behavior**
Optional, but powerful:
- How they behave in a tavern
- How they behave when confronted
- How they behave when alone with a PC
- How they behave when their secret is threatened

## **15. Escalation Path**
If the PCs push them:
- What they reveal first
- What they reveal under pressure
- What they reveal only if cornered
- What they will lie about
